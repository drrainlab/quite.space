# 1.0.18 — tables, formulas, and a long message that opens as a page

**Tables render.** A pasted comparison table — a header row over a line
of dashes — used to reflow into a bubble as pipes and dashes. It is a
table now, in chats and in posts, scrolling sideways when it is wider
than its column.

**Formulas render.** `$E=mc^2$` in a sentence, `$$ … $$` on its own
lines: the TeX people actually paste — subscripts and powers, fractions,
roots, `\text{…}`, Greek letters and the usual operators, sums and
integrals with their limits, `\left( … \right)`, accents, matrices and
cases — is translated into the browser's own math rendering. No library
and no fonts to download; nothing is fetched. What cannot be read is
shown as the characters the author typed, never dropped. Prices stay
prices: "$5 and $10" is not a formula.

**A long message is a preview.** Past seven hundred characters or
fourteen lines, a message keeps the top of its rendered text in the
bubble, fading out, with "read in full" under it. That opens the whole
message as a page with the article's typography — tables, formulas,
lists and quotes included — closed by the button, the backdrop or
Escape. Short messages are exactly as they were.

No relay change.
