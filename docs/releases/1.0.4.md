# 1.0.4 — where the seconds go

```
   ·  ✦   ·     minted ─10 ms─▶ relay ─127 ms─▶ ✓✓        ·   ✦
 ✦    ·     ·   the node measured it; nobody was asked   ·      ·
```

"Delivery feels slow" is now a number with a place. Every own message
carries a timeline the node measures on its own — when the frame was
minted here, when a relay first accepted it (and which one), when a
device outside your own hand first signed for holding it. Settings →
relay diagnostics shows the last three: *→ relay 21 ms (host) → ✓✓
137 ms*. Nothing crosses the wire for this; the three facts were already
this device's own.

Three seconds handed back along the way:

**The parked listener sleeps on the socket.** It used to check its
inbox every half second; now the connection wakes it the moment a packet
lands. The doorbell to a dark phone measured 623 ms → 237 ms — and a
phone in a pocket wakes for packets, not for timers.

**Pushes go to every relay at once.** They were walked in order, so one
relay slow to dial — throttling, or behind a VPN — held every other
recipient's copy hostage for the length of its timeout.

**The screen takes three extra looks after you press Send** (at 1, 3 and
6 seconds), whatever the window's focus. The regular poll is ten seconds
away when the window is not in front, and the person who just sent is
the one looking.

Measured on a two-node stand: a relay accepted in 10–21 ms, the double
checkmark came home in 128–144 ms.
