# 0.1.3 — four digits, and the key writes itself

```
   ·  ✦   ·        ╭──────────────╮       ·   ✦
 ✦    ·      ·     │  0 . 1 . 3   │  ·        ·
   ·    ✦          ╰──────────────╯     ✦   ·
```

Nobody should have to invent a passphrase under the pressure of a setup
screen — and everybody who does invents a bad one.

**⌨ A first run is now a name and four digits.** The key is drawn for you:
six words from a frozen list, 66 bits, never shown and never typed. The
digits wrap it; the passphrase underneath is stronger than anything a
person would have thought up while waiting to get in. The code is asked
for twice, because a mistyped one loses no data but loses the easy way
in — and you would find that out at the worst possible moment.

**◈ Carry your own key if you prefer.** The long way in is one click
away, on the same screen it always was, and it now confirms what you
typed before it makes anything.

**👤 On the phone, your face can open it.** Offered once, after you set a
code, never again. The key is minted so the hardware itself refuses to
decrypt until the sensor has just seen you — not a check the app makes
and could forget to. StrongBox where the handset has one. Adding a new
fingerprint to the phone destroys the seal, so nobody inherits it.

Turning it on **forgets the remembered passphrase**, and that is the
whole feature: an app that was already open before the sensor was asked
would be a gate with a hole beside it.

**⌁ The keypad has a feel now.** A tick under each digit, a different one
for a correction, a longer one for a refusal — through the system's own
touch feedback, so a phone told to stay quiet stays quiet.

**✧ Reactions: one gesture on every device.** A tap opens the palette
everywhere. Under a thumb the choice is two beats — the first tap on a
meaning plays it and picks it, the second sends — because a tap used to
win the race against the long-press timer and stamp the first slot
before anyone saw a choice. On a desktop, hover still previews.

**⚑ A phone paired on 0.1.2 could see nobody.** Its own words synced and
every room was empty: no history, no member cards, no media. One cause —
a computer that migrated remembers the friends it knew before devices
were certified, and a phone paired afterwards was born without that
memory, so those friends' messages sat in its hold forever waiting for
proof that will never exist. That knowledge belongs to the *person*, not
to one machine, and it now travels with you when you pair. **Pair again
after updating and the rooms fill in.**

⬇ **The interface lives in the binary** — needs the new build, not a
reload. macOS · Linux · Android.

⚠ **Still true:** four digits are hardware on the phone and a *shortcut*
on the desktop — a copied data folder is about an hour of guessing there,
and the passphrase remains the real secret · no media over LoRa · a relay
sees sizes and timing · public spaces are signed plaintext · no Windows
build · a lost keystore is a lost space.
