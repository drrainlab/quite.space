# 1.0.26 — a word leaves for good

**Sending is its own lane, and it carries only what is new.** For weeks a
message could sit on its dot for minutes — fifteen seconds on a good day —
and leave only when the app was reopened. The causes were found one under
the other on the owner's phone and on the stand, and then the ground under
them was rebuilt rather than patched (plan LT-4, `docs/plans/`).

- **The delta, on the express lane.** A word rides its own connection to
  the relay, chosen by the size of what the recipient still lacks, not by
  the size of the room's history. A newcomer's first copy of a long history
  goes on the bulk lane, in parallel, and never stands in front of a word.
- **A history never stands in front of a word.** A newcomer's copy of a
  long history goes to a courier on the bulk lane; the sending pass does
  not wait for it, in that room or in any other. The first pass after this
  update carries every room's history once, in the background, because the
  book below is new — before this release that walk happened on every open.
- **A restart re-mails nothing.** What this device has already put into
  which mailbox is now remembered on disk (a sealed document, keyed per
  mailbox), so a fresh process owes a quiet room nothing and dials nobody.
  The cursor moves only after the relay has accepted every copy of a
  batch; a crash between the acceptance and the note repeats a copy, never
  skips one. Once a day each mailbox gets a full offer anyway, because a
  relay keeps mail in memory and a restarted relay has forgotten it.
- **Nothing rides twice.** A presence or a stale reading that became part
  of somebody's chain used to ride to every mailbox on every pass, and a
  live presence rode on every pass too; on a phone with many rooms that
  was a dial to every mailbox every fifteen seconds, each one ringing the
  owner's other device, which answered, which rang back. Each is offered
  once per mailbox now, like everything else.
- **Your mail first.** The sync cycle collects this device's own mailbox
  before it pushes or reads the public world.
- **"Delivered" follows the other screen by a second, not by a cycle.**
  The receiving device signs for a frame when the frame arrives — from the
  pull that brought it, or over the local link when the author is in the
  same room — and a burst of messages is one receipt, not one per word.
  Between two devices on one Wi‑Fi the receipt never touches a relay.
- **One route chooser for everything.** Receipts, grants, knocks and
  revocations pick a peer's route the way messages do; a loopback address
  a peer once stated is neither recorded nor advertised outside a
  loopback world (the stand's stale `127.0.0.1` route was the last cause).
- **The screen says what the sender's lane is doing.** *Settings → Relay*
  shows the last outbox pass (how long, how long ago, which lane), the
  last five messages' timeline and the typical → relay → ✓✓ times, and
  refreshes itself while the sheet is open.

**A silent deafness, found on the way.** Two collects landing the same
item at the same moment (the cycle's pull and the doorbell's, each with a
copy a different member had re-offered) raced on one temporary file, and
the loser's error looked to the node like a dead disk — after which it
stopped collecting mail for good, without a word. The store now serialises
the writes, the node logs that latch with its cause, and a test holds the
door.

**Protocol.** One added sync message between live peers (delivery
receipts over a local link); older builds skip it. Relay wire unchanged.
The offer book is a local document; an older build ignores it, a newer
build without one starts with an empty book.

**Honest costs.** Each relay-borne receipt is one item in the author's
mailbox and therefore one ring of their doorbell, a second or so after
they typed; the debounce keeps a burst to one. Frames a relay lost (its
restart, or a recipient away longer than the relay keeps mail) are
offered again by the daily full pass — up to a day without a signal from
the relay. Not in this release: putting a batch into many mailboxes in one
round trip (needs the relays updated first), and an ingress this device
once listened on is still announced forever.

On the owner's phone, after three release candidates measured in one
night: the dot on a sent message goes in three to four seconds where it
took fifteen, and minutes after sleep.

Relays: no update required.
