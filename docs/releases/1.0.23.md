# 1.0.23 — a closed phone can still hear the bell

**Android: "something is waiting", with the app closed and your code still
guarding it.** Android closes apps — memory, a task manager, an update — and
brings our background service back in seconds. If your passphrase is sealed
behind a code or a face, which is the right choice, that service could not
reopen the node, and the phone heard nothing until you happened to open
Quiet. Measured on a handset: zero relay connections, and it stayed that way.

Now, while Quiet is open, it writes down the *addresses* of its own
mailboxes for the week ahead. When it is closed, a small watcher parks at
your relays on those addresses — holding **no key and no passphrase** — and
can learn exactly one thing: that something arrived. It shows one line,
once: *"Something is waiting — open to see it."* No space, no sender, no
count, because it does not know them. It cannot read a mailbox or empty it;
that takes a capability which is never written to the phone's storage. You
open the app, enter your code, and the node collects the mail as always.

What that list is, said plainly: not harmless, but a bounded right to
*watch*. Somebody who took the file could learn, for as long as it is
valid, that something arrived for this device — never what or from whom. So
it lives in the app's private storage, out of backups, and expires after a
week; the watcher tells the relay only the current addresses, never the
week ahead. If Quiet stays unopened for a week the line changes to *"Open
Quiet to keep background notifications working."*

**Quiet comes back by itself after the phone restarts and after the app
updates** (when "Stay connected" is on). It used to stay down until opened.

**Mail that arrived while nobody was listening is no longer met with
silence.** A listener that lost its network for an hour used to come back,
park again, and sit over a full mailbox until its next slow poll. The relay
now says in its acknowledgement — one bit, not which mailbox and not how
much — that something is already there. (All three official relays carry
this; older apps ignore the extra bit.)

**Your place is kept, on Android too.** 1.0.22 kept it in the browser's own
storage, which Android gives a fresh copy of on every launch — so the phone
remembered nothing and reopened on the first space of the list. It is kept
by the node now, sealed with the rest of what the node keeps for itself:
the app reopens on the room you closed it in, at the message you had read
up to.

**What is measured, and what is not.** On one phone (Nothing Phone 1,
Android 15): the restart after an update, the watcher parking with the node
closed, and the line appearing, were all observed. **Not yet observed:** a
reboot, a night in Doze, a network loss and return, and a six-hour address
rollover on a real device (the rollover logic is unit-tested). Android may
delay any app's network while the phone sleeps; *Settings → This device →
Why nothing may arrive* offers the battery exemption that helps, and
nothing here is a promise about seconds.

## Also here: everything that was meant to be 1.0.22

1.0.22 was tagged and never published — its build waited on a test run that
flaked. If you are coming from 1.0.21, this release also brings:

- **Android: the screen could be dragged sideways into emptiness, and
  twitched.** 1.0.21's own doing — a decorative layer in the header drifted
  past the edge of the screen. It is gone, and the shell cannot scroll at all.
- **"relay · issue" while everything plainly worked.** The light went red on
  any failure anywhere in a sync cycle — somebody else's relay, an official
  relay a VPN cannot reach. It is about *your own* relay now; the other
  endpoint's complaint is the chip's tooltip.
- **Settings → This device → Why nothing may arrive**: Android is not
  allowing notifications (with a button to the system setting), battery
  optimisation is on (with the system's own "run unrestricted?" question),
  the passphrase is not remembered.
- The permanent "staying connected" card had never told the truth — "No
  relay" over seven live connections. It reads the real state now and
  refreshes once a minute.
- **The list of spaces can be made wider** — drag its right edge. On a wide
  screen the orbit stands alone on the left of the header; "public · read
  only" moved into the space's panel; a few faint stars replaced the glow;
  each kind of space has two neighbouring hues and send, invite and your own
  bubbles run between them. Changing your name no longer fails when you have
  kept a read-only public space.

The full 1.0.22 note is in the repository: `docs/releases/1.0.22.md`.

Relays: updated. No protocol break — an added bit that older clients skip.
