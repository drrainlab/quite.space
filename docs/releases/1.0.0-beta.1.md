# 1.0.0-beta.1 — the formats hold still now

```
   ·  ✦   ·      ╭────────────────────╮      ·   ✦
 ✦    ·     ·    │  1 . 0 . 0 · beta  │  ·       ·
   ·    ✦        ╰────────────────────╯    ✦   ·
```

The number changed by a whole digit, so the first thing to say is what
it buys you — and it is not polish.

**Everything this build writes, every later 1.x will open.** Your log,
your backup, the passes you minted, the bundles you carried on a stick,
the certificates that admit your devices. There is no server here to
migrate you, which means a format is a promise or it is a trap: a log
written today is opened by a build shipped in two years, on a device
that never spoke to us in between, possibly carried there by a stranger
over a radio. From this release on, that is a commitment rather than an
intention — ninety signed schemas, the envelope, the on-disk layout, the
backup container, the relay's wire, the radio profile.

**And what is *not* frozen is written down just as plainly**, because a
promise with a vague edge gets quietly reinterpreted later: the local
HTTP API (it is this build's own way of talking to its own interface, on
loopback, behind a token — it was never a public API), the interface
itself, the projections your device computes for its own use, and the
things that can always be fetched again — map tiles, link previews. The
rule underneath: *what travels between devices or survives a restart is
frozen; what a device decides for itself is free.* It is all in
[ADR-033](../../adr/ADR-033-what-1-0-promises.md).

**The beta is not a hedge on any of that.** The formats are frozen
today. What "beta" marks is the reach of our evidence: this is proven by
construction — tests, measurements, live stands — and by daily use on
our own devices, but not yet by a lot of strangers on hardware we have
never seen. That is a real difference, and it belongs in the name.

---

Since 0.1.7, a space also became somewhere work can live.

**🔧 Things with a history.** A machine, a board, a pump — each is an
object with revisions, tasks and observations, and the record of what
happened to it stays with it. Chat can point *at* an object, so "the
bandsaw" in a conversation is the same bandsaw with the same history.

**🎛 Works, versions, and notes on a timecode.** Releases hold tracks,
tracks hold sessions; every mix you upload is a version that supersedes
the last one without erasing it, ★ marks what you are listening to now,
and a note can sit at 2:14 rather than merely near it.

**🧭 A field, and a map of claims.** Places and sectors, routes,
markers, check-ins — and positions that age honestly in front of you:
live, then stale, then unknown, because the alternative is a map that
says somebody is somewhere when all anyone holds is a statement from
forty minutes ago. Sharing your position is an explicit act with a
signed expiry, and the switch says exactly what it does. An SOS travels
on its own lane.

Every field event fits a single LoRa frame — not by assumption but by
measurement: the bound on a route's length is whatever a real
Field-authored revision actually fits, and a test pins the constant so
that when they disagree, the constant loses.

**🗺 Real maps under it, offline-first.** OpenStreetMap, restyled to the
dark, cached encrypted on your device so the places you have looked at
keep working with no network at all. It is off by default and the switch
tells you why: fetching tiles tells a server which part of the world is
on your screen. Your connectivity choice outranks it — offline means
offline, and the map falls back to its own paper rather than to a
spinner.

**🌱 A sensor can speak in public.** Instruments already joined private
spaces in 0.1.7; now a greenhouse can publish to a broadcast space,
where readings travel in the open like everything else there — bound as
an attested writer before its first frame, and honestly badged when it
is a simulation.

**🔔 And a sleeping phone can be woken** by a relay that knows only that
*something* arrived, never what.

---

The edges we know about are still named in the
[README](../../README.md): LoRa is proven on two boards and still young,
there is no general multi-hop mesh, and the desktop builds are signed by
no authority. Those are product edges. They are not reservations on
anything above.
