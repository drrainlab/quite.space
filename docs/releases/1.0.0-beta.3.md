# 1.0.0-beta.3 — delivered is about machines, read is about you

```
   ·  ✦   ·        your message ── · ── ✓ ── ✓✓          ·   ✦
 ✦    ·     ·      sending · relays · their device     ·      ·
```

Friends kept asking this project for read receipts. This release is the
answer the doctrine allows — and the part of the question it refuses,
said out loud.

**✓✓ Delivery receipts (DR-1).** Your own messages now wear a quiet
ladder in the timestamp corner: `·` still owed to a relay, `✓` every
routed relay took it, `✓✓` **delivered** — and delivered means exactly
one thing: *a device outside your own hand has signed for holding the
bytes.* The receipt that lights it is a machine's statement about a
machine — "device D holds author A's chain up to position P", signed by
D, dropped into A's own per-space mailbox. It is clamped to the chain's
real length (nobody signs for frames that do not exist), max-merged
(replays cannot regress it), and a sibling's receipt is excluded — your
own phone syncing must never light the checkmark meant for your friend.

**What never crosses the wire: whether you read.** No read cursors, no
"seen", no typing lights. Read-state stays where
[ADR-017](../../adr/ADR-017-local-attention.md) put it — on your
device. The receipt says a machine has the bytes; when a person looks at
them is nobody's telemetry.

**The switch.** Settings → *Delivery receipts*, default on, per device:
it governs what YOUR device signs for others. Turn it off and your
devices go mute — senders simply keep the single checkmark. Receiving is
always on: refusing to learn what somebody signed protects nobody.

**Old clients are fine.** The receipt rides a bundle key that pre-DR-1
decoders skip by contract (ADR-009): a beta.2 node drains it as an empty
bundle and loses nothing.

**🚪 The grant that waited at the wrong door.** A private space created
on one device could fail to appear on the paired other — first seen in
the field, then pinned in three regression tests. When the granting
device's route book holds no route for its sibling, the sealed grant
honestly waits at the grantor's OWN relay (reported as a guess); but the
sibling only knocked on its own doors. Two devices whose auto-selection
picked different relays could each wait forever at a door the other
never used. The fetch now also knocks on every relay a certified sibling
has stated — the grant comes home within a sync tick.

Both changes were verified live before this tag: the checkmark climbed
to ✓✓ within four seconds of the recipient's sync, and the stranded
private space arrived on a real phone the moment its build learned to
knock on its sibling's door.
