# Trademark policy

The code is open. The name is not, and the two are separate on purpose.

Apache-2.0 says so in its own section 6: the licence grants copyright and
patent rights and explicitly does **not** grant permission to use the
project's trade names, trademarks, service marks or product names. AGPL
grants no trademark rights either. This document says what that means in
practice, so that a fork knows exactly what it may and may not do.

## What is covered

- **quite.space** — the name and the domain
- **Quiet Spaces** — the project name
- **Terminal Network** — the network's name
- the logos and the visual identity
- the names of the official clients
- the **Official Relay** and **Verified Space** marks
- the official catalog and its listing

## What anyone may do, without asking

- use, modify, fork and redistribute the code, under its licence
- say truthfully that software is *built on*, *compatible with*, *speaks*
  or *implements* the Quiet Spaces protocol — nominative use is fine, and
  saying what your thing interoperates with is not a claim to be us
- run a relay, a bridge or a client of their own
- write about, teach, review or criticise the project by name
- keep the name in an unmodified redistribution — that is not a fork

## What needs permission

- naming a **modified** build, a fork or a derived product `quite.space`,
  `Quiet Spaces` or `Terminal Network`, or anything close enough to be
  mistaken for them
- using the logos or visual identity as the identity of your product
- presenting a service as an **Official Relay**, or a space as a
  **Verified Space**, when it has not been through whatever process those
  marks come to mean
- listing in the official catalog as though endorsed
- signing a release as though it came from the project
- suggesting endorsement, affiliation or partnership that does not exist

## The rule for forks, in one sentence

> **A modified build does not carry the official name, the logos or the
> release signatures without permission.**

Fork freely — that is what the licence is for. Give it your own name, so
that the person installing it knows whose build they are running. This is
not about controlling what people do with the code; it is about a person
being able to tell whether the relay they trust with their traffic is the
one they think it is. In a network whose entire security story rests on
knowing which key you are talking to, a name that can be borrowed is a
real attack, not a branding concern.

## Asking

Requests, and anything ambiguous, go to the address in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) until a dedicated contact exists. Permission,
where granted, will be in writing and specific.

## Status

This is the project's stated policy, not a registration, and not legal
advice. Trademark protection depends on use and, in most jurisdictions,
on registration; both should be reviewed with a lawyer before the first
public release, together with the licence texts and the CLA.
